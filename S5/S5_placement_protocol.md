## 1. Rules as frozen

Frozen: 2026-10-24 (time and SHA-256 recorded in the project log). Any later change must be a dated amendment at the end of this file.

The phases are indicative age bands: Phase 1 ≈ 11–13 (ISCED 2), Phase 2 ≈ 14–16 (ISCED 2/3 boundary), Phase 3 ≈ 16–18 (ISCED 3). They form a **hypothesised learning progression** (Corcoran, Mosher & Rogat 2009), not a validated one.

### Rules (applied to each competency × phase cell)
- **P1 Prerequisite.** A competency is not placed before the phase in which its mathematical and physics prerequisites appear in *standard* (non-advanced) secondary curricula. Evidence:
  - Stadermann et al. 2019 (curricula of 15 countries; QP mostly in final-year electives, ages 17–19);
  - Lehka 2021, Appendix Г pp. 223–229 (Ukrainian programmes: probability and complex numbers only in grade-10 advanced mathematics; no matrices in any extract);
  - curriculum facts reported in the included studies (screening.csv / evidence_matrix.csv).
- **P2 Representation demand.** Treatments are ordered as follows:
  - verbal/visual (phenomenological) →
  - probabilities and real-valued two-state descriptions tied to experiments (semi-quantitative) →
  - complex amplitudes and 2×2 matrices (formal; an optional Phase-3 extension only).

  Bouchée et al. (2022) give four reasons why quantum mechanics is difficult: (a) linking formalism to experience, (b) counter-intuitive phenomena, (c) the shift from deterministic to probabilistic thinking, (d) the limits of everyday language. They are used as **design constraints**, not as phases:
  - (b) and (d) → language and phenomena work first;
  - (c) → probabilistic reasoning is seeded early;
  - (a) → formalism only when anchored in an experiment (polarisation, Mach–Zehnder interferometer).
- **P3 Age-matched evidence.** An included study that taught the topic to learners within the phase's age band and reported outcomes makes the cell *evidence-supported*. A study with learners in an adjacent band makes it at most *plausible*. Age or grade must be **reported** in the study. Ages inferred from grade are marked "inferred" and use the country's school system.
- **P4 Consensus.** Expert or stakeholder consensus (Krijtenburg-Lewerissa et al. 2019; Merzel et al. 2024; Bitzenbauer et al. 2025; the printed stakeholder ratings in Lehka 2021, Tables 2.7–2.14) can support or oppose a placement but never places a topic on its own.
  - Bitzenbauer et al. 2025 act as a **ceiling**: advanced quantum-technology topics are treated conceptually, not mathematically, even in Phase 3.
  - Lehka's ratings are CFQT-derived items, so they are not independent of source S1; state this whenever they are used.

### Confidence grades
- **H:** P1 met + ≥1 age-matched study with outcomes (P3) + no contrary consensus.
- **M:** P1 met + adjacent-age evidence or supporting consensus.
- **L:** only judgement or stakeholder support, or contrary evidence present.
- **Conj (conjecture):** no age-matched or adjacent-age evidence. The cell is a design proposal to be tested.

### Evidence-type codes (matrix column `evidence_type`)
C = curriculum document; E = empirical classroom study with outcomes; A = adjacent-age empirical study; X = expert/stakeholder consensus; S = synthesis/review; J = authors' judgement.

### Use of the Lehka (2021) thesis
Descriptive only, with page numbers: curriculum coverage, course feasibility, printed ratings. **Never as validation of the phases.** [One internal sentence, which names a separate manuscript, is redacted here for anonymous review.]

### Grade definitions as frozen and as applied

The grade definitions frozen with the rules (above) were replaced by operational decision 1 (Amendment A1), written at 2026-10-25T20:53Z, before any cell was computed (the time-stamped placement log is published as S5, Part B). The frozen definitions overlapped (a cell with only supporting consensus was both M and L) and let consensus alone raise a cell to M, which rule P4 forbids; they also had no grade for cells in which the rules conflict.

| Grade | As frozen (2026-10-24) | As applied (A1, 2026-10-25) |
|---|---|---|
| H | P1 met + at least one age-matched study with outcomes (P3) + no contrary consensus | Prerequisites met or not needed + at least one age-matched study that taught and tested the element and reported outcomes + no opposing consensus |
| M | P1 met + adjacent-age evidence or supporting consensus | As H but with an opposing consensus, or with adjacent-age evidence only |
| L | Only judgement or stakeholder support, or contrary evidence present | Contrary evidence (a null or negative outcome for an element that a study taught and tested) |
| Conj | No age-matched or adjacent-age evidence | No age-matched or adjacent-age evidence, whatever the consensus |
| U | (not defined) | Evidence exists, but the prerequisite and representation rules conflict, or no curricular source documents when a prerequisite is taught |

No minimum sample size, design or reporting standard is set for H: one age-matched study that taught and tested the element and reported outcomes suffices. The matrix (S5_placement_matrix.csv) gives the records behind every grade, so each H can be weighed by its sample.

## 2. Amendments
Everything above this heading is the frozen text (full-file SHA-256 at freezing fd79e9529043392f…; SHA-256 of the part above this heading 1886be2930fcb463…). Amendments are appended below, dated, and say whether they were made before or after any placement result was seen.

### A1 — operational decisions, fixed 2026-10-25T20:53Z, BEFORE any cell was computed (Section 3 below; recorded here 2026-10-26)
- **Grade precedence and an "undecided" grade.** The grade definitions above do not cover a placement whose rules conflict. A placed cell is graded, in this order: Conj if there is no age-matched or adjacent [taught+tested] evidence; **undecided** if P1 is not met but P2 admits the cell as an optional Phase-3 extension, or if no P1 source documents the curricular phase of a prerequisite; L if contrary evidence is present; H if P1 is met (or there is no prerequisite), age-matched evidence exists, and no consensus source opposes; M if the same holds but a consensus source opposes, or if the evidence is adjacent only.
- **No prerequisite** (a verbal or visual treatment that shows its own phenomenon) counts as P1 met. Sensitivity "na_neq_met" reports the alternative.
- **Overlap in the grade definitions.** "M … or supporting consensus" and "L: only judgement or stakeholder support" overlap with "Conj: no age-matched or adjacent-age evidence". Following P4 (consensus "never places a topic on its own") and the brief of the placement run of 2026-10-25, written before the matrix was filled, consensus or stakeholder support alone never raises a grade above Conj, and L is used only for contrary [taught+tested] evidence.
- Further operational decisions (representation matching, grade-only records, mixed samples, meanings of P4 support/neutral/oppose, adjacency) are items 2–12 of Section 3 below; the build script reports a sensitivity scenario for each.

### A2 — correction of the P1 evidence statement, 2026-10-26, AFTER the matrix was built
- Lehka (2021) Appendix Г is "Extracts from the curricula for grades 5–11" and spans pp. 223–233, not 223–229. Its mathematics extracts cover grades 10–11 only: the standard level shows coordinates and vectors; the advanced (pogliblenyi) grade-10 course shows probability theory (axioms, conditional probability) and complex numbers; the profile grade-11 course shows probability theory. Its only grade 5–9 extract is informatics (grade 8). Matrices appear in no extract.
- Because the appendix is a selection of extracts, it shows where these topics appear in the courses quoted. It does **not** show that probability is absent from standard courses, and it says nothing about lower-secondary mathematics. The frozen sentence "probability and complex numbers only in grade-10 advanced mathematics" is therefore corrected to: "in the extracts reproduced, probability theory appears in the advanced grade-10 and profile grade-11 courses and complex numbers in the advanced grade-10 course; no extract covers lower-secondary mathematics, and matrices appear in none".
- Effect on the matrix: none. The build already treated elementary probability in Phases 1–2 as "not established" (no P1 source documents it), so the cells that use it are undecided under A1.

### A3 — five amendments adopted by the authors, 2026-10-26, AFTER the grades were seen
The authors adopted proposals AM-2 to AM-6 after seeing the grades. The grid reported in the manuscript applies A1–A3; the grid under A1–A2 only (the "as frozen" grid) is reported in Section 4 of this supplement alongside it.
- **AM-2: a lower-secondary mathematics source for P1.** The standard national mathematics programme for grades 5–9 of the same system as the Lehka (2021) extracts is added as a P1 source (Ministry of Education and Science of Ukraine, *Matematyka. 5–9 klasy*, approved by order No. 804 of 7 June 2017). Its grade-9 algebra course, Topic 4, introduces "the frequency and probability of a random event". Elementary probability (relative frequency, probability of a random event) is therefore documented in a standard curriculum. Grade 9 is the last year of lower secondary (pupils aged about 14–15). By the school-level mapping applied to the other curricular grades (grade-8 informatics is Phase 1; grade 10, the first year of upper secondary, is Phase 2) it falls in Phase 1. By age it falls in Phase 2. The build counts the prerequisite as available from Phase 1, but no grade depends on the choice, because no Phase-1 placement has elementary probability as a prerequisite. For the same reason, naming the English (key stage 3) or US (Common Core grade 7) mathematics curriculum instead would give the same grades. Effect: 13 Phase-2 placements go from undecided to H and 4 to M.
- **AM-3: optional formal extensions.** Where P2 admits an optional Phase-3 formal extension and an advanced or profile mathematics course supplies its prerequisite, the prerequisite counts as met, conditionally ("met, conditional"). Effect: complex amplitudes in Phase 3 go from undecided to Conj (no study taught them).
- **AM-4: entry.** A unit's earliest admissible phase is its entry, and it is placed in every later phase. This states what the build already did; no grade changes.
- **AM-5: Dirac notation.** Dirac notation with real coefficients counts as a real-valued two-state description (semi-quantitative). This states what the build already did; no grade changes.
- **AM-6: the framework's own placements as prerequisites.** Where no curricular source documents a prerequisite by a phase, the prerequisite counts as met if this framework itself places, by that phase, a unit whose treatment teaches it, and that unit's own prerequisites are met there (no circularity). This applies only to energy levels (the energy-level diagrams of "Energy levels and line spectra", from Phase 2) and matter waves (the de Broglie wavelength, Phase 3 only). Effect: atomic clocks in Phase 2 go from undecided to Conj (no study taught them).
- **Correction to A2.** A2 said the build already treated elementary probability in Phases 1–2 as "not established". In fact, the prerequisite catalogue recorded it as "not met", citing the frozen sentence that A2 corrects. Both statuses gave "undecided" under A1, so no grade was affected. The catalogue now records "not established" with the corrected basis, and the "as frozen" grid uses that status.

All four PDFs have the same DOIs, and every body paragraph and every extracted number matches. ## 3. Operational decisions (fixed before any cell was graded)

The rules were applied literally. Where they were silent or ambiguous, the decisions below were fixed before any cell was computed; each has a sensitivity scenario in Section 4 where it could change a grade.

1. **Grade precedence and the undecided grade** (Amendment A1).
2. **Prerequisites are tools, not topics.** For each element and level of representation, the prerequisites are the mathematical tools and classical-physics concepts that the treatment uses but does not itself introduce. The earliest phase in which any standard (non-advanced, non-profile) curriculum among the sources documents a prerequisite is that prerequisite's phase; later or non-standard appearances are recorded as dissent. Curricular grades are mapped to phases by school level (for example, a first year of upper secondary falls in Phase 2), and the mapping is marked as inferred.
3. **Conflicting rules.** Where the representation rule admits an optional Phase-3 formal extension but no standard curriculum supplies its prerequisite (complex amplitudes; matrices where no source documents them), the cell is placed and graded undecided, with the conflict stated. Amendment A3 (AM-3) later counted such a prerequisite as met, conditionally, where an advanced or profile course supplies it.
4. **Grades without ages.** A band is inferred from a reported grade only when the study names its country or school system; that system's grade-to-age mapping is used, and the band is marked as inferred and never reported as the study's age. Sensitivity scenarios S-A and S-B.
5. **Special samples.** A sample that mixes school and first-year university learners without separate school outcomes counts as adjacent evidence only (scenario O5alt counts it as age-matched). A study reporting several year groups credits each phase only with what the year group of that phase was taught and tested on.
6. **Meaning of consensus.** Support = the item met the source's own inclusion criterion. Neutral = below that criterion without a consensus to exclude. Oppose = an explicit rejection: high consensus with inclusion below half, or the expert study's ceiling against mathematical treatment of advanced quantum-technology topics. None of the consensus sources states that it concerns ages 11–13, so consensus support is recorded for Phases 2–3 only; an opposition applies to all phases. The stakeholder ratings in the thesis are derived from the workforce framework's items and are not independent of source S1. Scenario S-C reads every below-criterion item as opposed.
7. **Contrary evidence** must meet the same bar as support (taught and tested). Only a null or negative outcome, or a comparison in which the group without the element did better, counts. An element that a study taught but did not test is context, not contrary evidence.
8. **Elements without a topic code in the evidence matrix** are matched by stated rules (for example, real-valued two-state descriptions require a real-vector representation together with a two-state topic that was taught and tested, with real amplitudes). Programming has no topic code, so no study can support it; this is a stated limit.
9. **Placement.** The prerequisite rule sets the earliest phase, the representation rule sets the treatment in each phase, and the expert ceiling keeps quantum-technology topics conceptual. The rules paraphrase the expert study loosely. The study found support for teaching foundational topics conceptually rather than mathematically and judged more advanced quantum-technology topics unsuitable for secondary school; its panel stressed that the result should not limit future curricula. Keeping quantum-technology topics conceptual is therefore a design choice of this framework, applied as "no mathematical treatment of quantum-technology topics". An element is placed in every phase from its earliest admissible phase (Amendment A3, AM-4, states this in the rules).
10. **Awareness and formal entries** (the paper's Table 5) follow the two lowest proficiency levels of the workforce framework: awareness = what a technology does and why it matters; formal = an explanation of how it works, at any level of representation.
11. **Grid cells** carry one grade per listed element; grades are never merged upwards.
12. **Adjacency.** A study is age-matched for a phase if its (reported or inferred) age band intersects that phase's band, which overlap at 16; it is adjacent if it intersects only a neighbouring phase's band. A further check lists the Phase-1 placements whose adjacent evidence comes only from learners aged 15 or older.
